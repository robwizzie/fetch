# FETCH — Game Plan

This document records the decisions behind the prototype and the plan to turn it into a full party game.
Reference: the two design boards (title/menu, dog select, toys, modes, arenas, game flow) and Boomerang Fu.

## 1. Engine and language: Godot 4.7 + GDScript

**Decision: Godot 4.7.2, GDScript, 3D with a fixed tilted camera (Boomerang Fu style).** Unity was the stated preference; here is why Godot wins for this
specific project, and when to reconsider.

| | Godot 4.7 (GDScript) | Unity 6 (C#) |
|---|---|---|
| Fit for a low-poly 3D top-down party game | Very good. `CharacterBody3D`, `Area3D`, primitive meshes, `Label3D`, `CPUParticles3D`, directional shadows and toon shading cover everything Boomerang Fu does. Jolt physics is the default. | Excellent; this is Unity's home turf too. URP + ProBuilder for prototyping. |
| Local multiplayer input | Every `InputEvent` carries a `device` index; polling per device is ~100 lines (`scripts/input/device_input.gd`). No package needed. | Needs the Input System package + `PlayerInputManager`; workable, more ceremony. |
| Iterating with Claude Code / AI | Everything is plain text: scenes (`.tscn`), resources (`.tres`), scripts. The whole game can be written, diffed, reviewed and **run headless in CI** without opening an editor. This repo's smoke test proves a full match plays out. | Scenes and prefabs are YAML with GUID references; generated by the editor, painful to author or review by hand. No headless test path without the editor + licenses. |
| Cost / licensing | MIT, no fees, no install/revenue terms, ever. | Free under the revenue threshold, but the 2023 pricing episode is a real risk for a commercial party game. |
| Console ports | Not built in. Switch/PlayStation/Xbox go through a porting partner (W4 Games "W4 Consoles", Pineapple Works) or a licensed contractor. | Built in with platform licenses. |
| Steam | GodotSteam (GDExtension) covers achievements, Steam Input, Remote Play Together; PC export is one click. | Steamworks.NET. Equivalent. |
| Editor / tooling | Small, fast, opens in seconds. Weaker asset store than Unity. | Huge ecosystem, asset store, more tutorials. |
| Language | GDScript (Python-like, fast to write; C# is also available in the .NET build if you want it). | C# only. |

Reconsider Unity only if a console launch on day one is non-negotiable *and* you'd rather own the port than
contract it. For "ship on Steam, then port if it sells" (the usual indie path, including Boomerang Fu's), Godot
is the faster road, and the codebase is small enough that a later port is a rewrite of a few thousand lines.

**Why 3D.** The reference boards and Boomerang Fu both read as a chunky, toy-like 3D world seen from a
tilted camera: props have volume, dogs cast shadows, balls arc over things. That look is hard to fake in 2D
and cheap to get in 3D with low-poly assets. Gameplay is still 2D underneath: dogs and toys move on the
ground plane (XZ), toys fly at a fixed height, so the physics stays as simple and fair as a 2D game.
The prototype's placeholder art is built from primitives (capsules, spheres, boxes) with toon shading and an
inverted-hull outline; each breed has its own body plan (`DogModel.breed_spec`) so Shadow is a black Lab,
Goose a grey pit bull, Luna a tri-colour corgi, Hattie a black-and-tan dachshund and Posey a golden retriever.
Real models drop in by replacing two nodes (`DogModel`, `ToyModel`) and the prop builder (`Obstacle`).
Menus follow the boards: the FETCH wordmark (built in code from Luckiest Guy with a paw in the C and a tennis
ball streak), wood-plank buttons with paw icons over the live 3D backyard, per-dog card colours on select.

## 2. Starting point

Searched for an open-source Boomerang Fu clone or a "top-down arena party game" template to fork. Findings:

- **No Boomerang Fu clone exists** in the open (Godot or Unity). Nothing to fork.
- **[Starship Olympics](https://github.com/notapixelstudio/starship-olympics)** (Godot 4.4, MIT code, CC-BY-NC-SA art)
  is the closest real thing: a shipped 1–4 player top-down local party game with 20+ minigames. Too big and
  too specific to fork, but it is the best **reference codebase** for party-game structure (minigame
  selection, scoring across games, joining flow). Read it when building extra modes and the campaign.
- **[stesproject/godot-2d-topdown-template](https://github.com/stesproject/godot-2d-topdown-template)** (MIT, tested on 4.7.2)
  is an RPG/adventure template: dialogues, inventory, saves. Wrong genre; skip.
- Unity options ([InputSystem_Warriors](https://github.com/UnityTechnologies/InputSystem_Warriors),
  [molleindustria/local-multiplayer-unity](https://github.com/molleindustria/local-multiplayer-unity)) only solve input, not the game.

**Decision: build a clean, data-driven scaffold ourselves** (this repo). The core loop of a Boomerang Fu-like
is small (~1,500 lines here), and starting clean means every system is shaped for *this* game: dogs, toys,
arenas and modes as data files, one input abstraction, a signal bus, and a headless smoke test from day one.

## 3. What's in the prototype

Everything listed in the design board's MVP:

- Top-down movement with acceleration, dash with invincibility frames and cooldown
- Tennis ball throw with wall bounces, friction, "dangerous" speed threshold, thrower immunity until first bounce
- Catch: press with a toy nearby, or press slightly early (buffered `catch_window`), with a miss cooldown
- One-hit elimination, drop your toy on death
- 2–4 players on any mix of gamepads and two keyboard layouts; press-to-join dog select
- Arenas: Backyard (doghouse, crates, table, pool that slows you) and Living Room (couch, TV, armchairs, rug)
- Round manager: countdown, round winner banner, first-to-N points, results screen
- All 5 dogs with distinct stats, 6 toys as stat variants (2 flagged prototype), 7 modes listed (1 playable)
- Placeholder juice: screen shake, hit-stop, particles, floating "BONK!/CATCH!", synthesised SFX
- Headless smoke test + GitHub Actions CI

## 4. Roadmap

Each phase is a playable build. Do them in order; every phase makes the game more fun to test.

### Phase 1 — Feel (1–2 weeks)
- Tune numbers with real people on real pads: speeds, dash, catch window, ball speed, arena size.
- Round timer + sudden death (shrinking arena or extra balls) so rounds can't stall.
- Pause menu (any player), rematch shortcut, "press A to skip" on results.
- Toy specials: Rope Toy knockback, Squeaky Chicken squeak ping, Super Ball extra ricochet, Bone slow-but-lethal.
- Simple bots using `DeviceInput.VIRTUAL` so 1 player can practise (the smoke test already has a crude one).

### Phase 2 — Content (2–4 weeks)
- Modes: Pack Battle (2v2, teams in `PlayerSlot`), Golden Ball (hold-to-score), Fetch Frenzy (bring toys home),
  Treat Hunt, Squirrel! (an AI-driven pickup that runs away). Each is one `GameMode` subclass + one `.tres`.
- Arenas: Beach (waves push the ball), Rooftop (fall off the edge = out), Snow Day (slippery floor). Each is one scene.
- Modifiers for Custom Match (ball speed, toy count, low gravity for jumps, one-toy-only), stored in `Game`.

### Phase 3 — Art and audio (parallel with 2)
- Replace `DogModel` with a rigged low-poly dog (idle, run, throw, catch, dash, KO) via `AnimationPlayer`, keep the same API.
- Replace `Obstacle` kinds with real props (glTF), keep the collision footprints. Consider a `Y-sort`-free world: it's 3D, depth just works.
- Arena dressing: fences, trees, house wall in the background, skybox; the camera sees outside the arena.
- Title screen and menus matching the boards (wood-panel buttons, dog portraits, "Same friends. New tricks.").
- Real SFX + music; the `Sfx.play("name")` calls stay, only the streams change.

### Phase 4 — Ship-ready
- Settings: volume, fullscreen, rebinding (Godot InputMap), rumble.
- Steam via GodotSteam: achievements, Remote Play Together (free "online" for a local game), Steam Input.
- Campaign / Party Play sequences (rotating modes with overall scoring), like Boomerang Fu's "Party Mode".
- Localization (`.csv` → Godot translations), accessibility (colour-blind rings, screen shake toggle).
- Console port evaluation with a partner once Steam numbers justify it.

## 5. Tools and assets

**Tools (all free):** Godot 4.7.2; Aseprite ($20) or LibreSprite/Krita for sprites; Audacity for sound;
[jsfxr](https://sfxr.me/) / [ChipTone](https://sfbgames.itch.io/chiptone) for quick SFX; Inkscape for vector props;
GodotSteam for Steam; Godot's built-in profiler and debugger.

**Placeholder → real art sources (check licenses per pack):**
- [Kenney](https://kenney.nl) — CC0 3D kits: *Furniture Kit*, *Nature Kit*, *City Kit*, *Food Kit* (arena props, toys), *UI Pack*, *Game Icons*.
- [Quaternius](https://quaternius.com) — CC0 low-poly packs including animated animals and props; the closest free match to the Boomerang Fu look.
- [Poly Pizza](https://poly.pizza) — searchable CC0/CC-BY low-poly models (dogs, furniture, backyard props).
- [itch.io "low-poly" 3D assets](https://itch.io/game-assets/tag-low-poly) — cheap stylised animal/pet packs.
- Blender (free) for tweaks, recolours and simple rigs; export glTF, Godot imports it natively.
- Fonts: Google Fonts (OFL) — *Fredoka*, *Baloo 2*, *Luckiest Guy*, *Bangers* match the board's chunky look.
- Audio: Kenney audio packs (CC0), [freesound.org](https://freesound.org) (filter CC0), dog barks recorded from your own dogs.
- Music: Kevin MacLeod (CC-BY), or commission; party games live on a strong 60–90s loop per arena.

For a commercial release the recommended path is commissioning one 3D artist for the 5 dogs (one shared rig,
6–8 animations) and re-using Kenney/Quaternius props for arenas; the dogs are the visual identity and the
cheapest place to win.

## 6. Risks and how the code handles them

- **Catch timing feel** is the whole game. It's isolated in `Dog._throw_or_catch`, `Dog.hit_by` and three numbers per dog. Tune there.
- **Ricochet chaos vs. fairness**: `ToyData.max_bounces` halves speed after N bounces; `danger_speed` makes slow toys harmless.
- **Input edge cases** (pad unplugged mid-match): handle `Input.joy_connection_changed` in `Game` (not done yet).
- **Performance** is a non-issue at this scale; the GL Compatibility renderer is on for integrated GPUs and web builds. Switch to Forward+ later if you want SSAO/SDFGI for a richer look on desktop.
- **Toys escaping the arena**: releases are ray-checked against walls and the match has a safety net that returns any escaped toy.
